“It's a dangerous business, Frodo, going out your door. You step onto the road, and if you don't keep your feet, there's no knowing where you might be swept off to.”
  <div style="text-align:right; margin-top:6px;">— Frodo Baggins</div>
</blockquote>

We were a group of six university students, setting out on what would become our first true “homies trip.”

For years this journey had lived inside our group chat. It was proposed enthusiastically, postponed endlessly, and resurrected every few months with renewed determination. Somehow, between assignments, exams, placements, internships, and life’s chaos, the plan finally escaped the chat and became real.

What followed was three days in the misty hills of Sittong. It was a trip filled with tea (pun-intended), wandering roads, loyal mountain dogs, and the quiet magic of the mountains.

## Into the Silent Hills

<blockquote class="highlight">
“In my restless dreams, I see that town…”
  <div style="text-align:right; margin-top:6px;">— From the letter to James Sunderland, Silent Hill 2</div>
</blockquote>

We reached NJP (New Jalpaiguri) around 11 in the morning, where our driver and guide, Santosh bhai, was already waiting for us. After a quick breakfast to shake off the train fatigue, we began our journey toward the hills.

Leaving Siliguri behind, the landscape slowly transformed.
The bustle of the city softened into rural plains, which gradually gave way to endless plantations. Soon enough, the roads began to curl around the hills, and we entered the misty, quiet world of Sittong.

On the way we stopped at a small roadside spot for some good old Darjeeling tea and steaming momos — the unofficial welcome ritual to the hills. Our first stops included Hanuman Top, where the hills gave way to a quaint little Hanuman Mandir and Kurseoung's official viewpoint.

From there we visited the Dow Hill School, a place famous not only for its colonial architecture but also for its eerie reputation and haunting legends. It's sister school, the Victoria Boys School has been closed owing appearances of these omnious sightings. The Gothic Architecture along with the thick mist that surronding the place, certainly gives of a Silent-Hills-esque vibe to the place.  Whether one believes the stories or not, the surrounding forest certainly carries a mysterious stillness.

## Sightseeing Around Sittong


<blockquote class="highlight">
“Not all those who wander are lost.”
  <div style="text-align:right; margin-top:6px;">— Gandalf, in the The Fellowship of the Ring</div>
</blockquote>

I woke up early the next morning hoping to catch the sunrise and perhaps a glimpse of Kanchenjunga. But the mountains had other plans. The mist had wrapped the entire valley in soft white silence. Oddly enough, the absence of the sunrise made the morning even more peaceful. The hills looked dreamy, almost suspended in time. Before leaving for the day, we took a small morning walk around our homestay, breathing in the crisp mountain air.

Our first destination was the Mahal Diram Tea Estate, arguably one of the most scenic places in Sittong. The rolling green plantations seemed endless, layered beautifully against the hills. The clouds' union with the hills gave it a very asthetic vibe, perfect for clicking cinematic shots.

<figure>
  <img
    src="https://github.com/aryapandit200408/blog/blob/master/src/img/sittong_trip/photo_9.png?raw=true"
     alt="The beautiful MDN logo."
     style="float: center; height: 40%; width: 40%;">
  <figcaption></figcaption>
</figure>

Next we stopped at an orange orchard. Unfortunately the season had ended about a month earlier, and the trees stood quietly without their bright fruits. Even so, the place had its own charm. From there we headed to Ahaldhara 360° Viewpoint, one of the most popular spots in the region. As the name suggests, the viewpoint offers breathtaking panoramic views of Sittong and the surrounding hills. On a clear day one would have a full panoramic view of the area and could even see the Teesta River in all its glory.

<figure>

Our next stop was Namthing Pokhri, a lake that had dried up due to the dry season, leaving behind an arid lakebed beside a small Shiva temple. Later we visited the Upper Ghayletar Longho Lepcha Buddhist Monastery, a serene and peaceful place perched quietly among the hills.

## A Dog of the Seven Mountains

<blockquote class="highlight">
“It wasn’t just a puppy.”
  <div style="text-align:right; margin-top:6px;">— John Wick</div>
</blockquote>

Day three was supposed to be our “rest day,” with no sightseeing planned and no fixed itinerary to follow. But freedom from plans often leads to the most unexpected and memorable moments.

We decided to spend the day simply wandering around the hills in search of whatever quiet beauty we might stumble upon. While casually browsing Google Maps, we noticed a small monastery tucked away somewhere in the hills, and naturally decided to hike our way there.

<figure>
  <img
    src="https://github.com/aryapandit200408/blog/blob/master/src/img/sittong_trip/photo_15.jpg?raw=true"
     alt="The beautiful MDN logo."
     style="float: center; height: 40%; width: 40%;">
  <figcaption>A hike though the mountains</figcaption>
</figure>

The walk itself became the highlight of the day.
The narrow mountain paths wound gently through misty slopes and patches of forest, the cool air fresh and invigorating. Everywhere we looked, the landscape offered something quietly beautiful — rolling hills, drifting clouds, and that peaceful stillness that only mountain villages seem to have.

The monastery itself was quaint and modest, yet incredibly charming, sitting quietly amidst the hills.

<figure>
  <img
    src="https://github.com/aryapandit200408/blog/blob/master/src/img/sittong_trip/photo_12.jpg?raw=true"
     alt="The beautiful MDN logo."
     style="float: center; height: 40%; width: 40%;">
  <figcaption>The quaint monastery</figcaption>
</figure>

Along the way we met a local resident who kindly invited us to his home and pointed us toward a hidden forest trail leading to a lesser known viewpoint. His warmth and hospitality added another unexpected moment to our wandering day.

And throughout this entire adventure, we were not alone.

Two mountain dogs from our homestay had decided to accompany us. They walked with us the whole way — sometimes beside us, sometimes ahead as if guiding the path. When we briefly lost our way, they seemed to know exactly where to go.

At one point, when one of our friends had to return early to the homestay due to some urgent work, one of the dogs actually accompanied him back, faithfully guiding him along the winding trail.

It felt strangely magical — like the mountains themselves had sent us guides.

<figure>
  <img
    src="https://github.com/aryapandit200408/blog/blob/master/src/img/sittong_trip/photo_18.jpg?raw=true"
     alt="The beautiful MDN logo."

That night, we gathered outside for a barbecue roast under the open night sky, sharing food, laughter, and stories.

The next morning arrived too quickly. We packed our bags, checked out of the homestay, and began the journey back down the hills to catch our trains — carrying with us memories of misty roads, quiet monasteries, and a little mountain magic.

## Into the Wild (for a Weekend)

The next morning we packed our bags and began the journey back, leaving the hills behind in order to catch our trains.Looking back, the trip felt almost like a coming-of-age chapter the kind that quietly marks a moment in life. There were no grand adventures or dramatic stories. Just misty hills, wandering roads, unexpected kindness from strangers, and the quiet companionship of friends.

And somehow, those simple moments made the entire journey feel magical.
