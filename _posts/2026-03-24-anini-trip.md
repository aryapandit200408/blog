## An offbeat part of Arunachal Pradesh

Arunachal Pradesh the largest of India's North Eastern Sister states is one with a rich blend of history, culture, food, and environmental beauty. It is home to over 26 major and 110 sub-tribes each with its distinct tradition customs and language. Although the more visited parts are its Western parts of Tawang and Ziro, we decided to explore the Dibang Valley a more offbeat but untouched part of the state. 

The range of hills in Lower Dibang Valley district of Arunachal Pradesh are inhabitated by the Idu Mishmi Tribe. As you would have guessed, these are known as Mishmi Hills. Renowned for their rich biodiversity, these hills attract wildlife enthusiasts and birdwatchers from across the country.

## Day 1: Journey from Kolkata to Roing via Dibrugarh
We begun our journey from Kolkata on the fine morning of 7th March and took a flight to Dibrugarh. Reaching Dibrugarh at 10:30 AM, from there we took the road to Roing via Namsai. The road was beautiful and well made, lined with tea gardens and small hills serving as a pleasant precendent of what was yet to come.

We then reached Namsai at around 1:00 PM and visited its famous Golden Pagoda. The Golden Pagoda was built in 2010 and stands as a beautiful symbol of Buddhist culture in the region. Its stunning golden structure, inspired by Thai architecture, gleamed under the afternoon sun, creating a serene and spiritual atmosphere. Surrounded by well-maintained gardens and prayer halls, the pagoda offered a peaceful retreat from the journey.

On the way we passed through the Mayodia Pass which is the highest point of Mishmi Hills perching at around 2,655 meters (8,711 feet) above sea level. It is known for receiving heavy snowfall between November and February making it a poupular tourist hub. The route from Roing to Mayodia is also very famous for Bird Watching.

## Day 3: The highlight of the trip - Acheso and Dree Valley
From Anini we went deeper into Dree valley to the small village of Acheso. Acheso is about 40 Kms from Anini and is famous for its 
emerald-green Dree River, waterfalls, lush pine forests, bamboo groves and sights of Mithuns.

## Day 4: Return to Roing and start of the second part of our trip
The day after, we undertook the trip back to Roing. Retracing back the way via Anini, Angolin, Etalin, all the way to Mayodia, the cool comfort of the Mishmi Hills made the 240+ Km trip bearable. 

We reached Roing City at around 6 PM and took a stroll through the Bazaar area. 

This marked the end of the first leg of our journey through Arunachal Pradesh and the beginning of the second part in Assam. See you in the next blog!
